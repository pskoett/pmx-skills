# Team Context Update

Help a team understand what may change its work, without duplicating the task board or exposing confidential discussions.

## Gather only relevant context

Start with information already supplied and any explicitly selected sources. Ask about audience, channel, language, period, and sensitivity if not established. Do not assume a particular employer, role, communication style, weekday, or platform. No integrations or other skills are required.

Briefly summarize what is known, then ask focused questions about gaps, one at a time or in a small related batch:

- External agreements, budget, or vendor changes that affect the team.
- Incoming requests or new work that might displace current plans.
- Leadership decisions or direction changes the audience needs to understand.
- Cross-team dependencies and decisions that are blocked or newly resolved.
- New evidence affecting goals, scope, confidence, or customer understanding.
- Anything else likely to surprise the team if left unsaid.

Skip categories with no news and do not re-ask answered questions. Stop when the useful context is covered; a quiet period may require only a short note or no update.

## Select and draft

1. **Filter for relevance.** Keep items that change understanding, preparation, or action. Omit routine visible task progress unless there is new context behind it. Do not pad with vague descriptions of being busy.
2. **Clarify implications.** For each item state what changed, what it means for the audience, any real action, and what remains unknown. Separate approved decisions from possibilities and ongoing discussions.
3. **Check disclosure.** Confirm whether negotiations, personnel matters, customer details, or unreleased decisions may be shared with this audience. Omit sensitive details pending clarification. Even saying a confidential discussion exists can disclose too much; do not invent a sanitized teaser without permission.
4. **Choose the format.** Write short paragraphs or bullets suited to the channel and user's preferences. Let themes emerge from content, not a mandatory category list. Use a date or period label when useful, but avoid a fixed length or compulsory greeting, emoji, or signoff.
5. **Review for surprises.** Does each item help the audience understand a change? Are dates and commitments supported? Did the draft imply certainty where the input was tentative?

Use a neutral, clear style unless the user supplies preferences. If they specifically request their own voice, use writing samples or optionally `ghost-writer` when available; its absence must not block a neutral draft. Keep fact selection and privacy checks here rather than delegating them to voice matching.

## Output

Provide the copyable message, with unresolved questions outside it. Do not embed editorial comments or confidential notes in a supposedly ready-to-send message. If a sensitive question prevents safe drafting, offer only the non-sensitive portion and explain the gap separately. Drafting does not authorize sending, scheduling a recurring update, or modifying shared plans.

## Synthetic example

The board already shows a migration in progress. A confirmed dependency change gives the team two fewer weeks to finish. Include the changed constraint and its consequence, not a recap of completed migration tasks. If an unapproved budget proposal is also mentioned, do not present it as a decided cut.
