# Product Planning Interview

Elicit the minimum product context needed to replace planning guesses with explicit decisions, then synthesize a usable brief.

## Workflow

1. **Inventory what is known.** Extract the problem, affected users, evidence, desired outcome, constraints, and decisions from the conversation or supplied documents. Reuse answers; never ask the user to repeat them.
2. **Audit planning gaps.** Identify assumptions that would materially alter scope or recommendation. Cover, as relevant: user and need, current behavior, scope boundaries and non-goals, dependencies, timing or resources, risk tolerance, success criteria, and evidence quality.
3. **Interview in short rounds.** Ask two or three focused questions per turn, ordered by decision impact. Prefer questions that expose tradeoffs over exhaustive questionnaires. Offer answer choices when they reduce effort without constraining a legitimate response.
4. **Reflect and adapt.** Briefly summarize consequential answers and update the next questions. Resolve contradictions directly. Distinguish facts, preferences, hypotheses, and unknowns; do not invent an answer or silently convert ambiguity into scope.
5. **Know when to stop.** End when the brief supports the next planning decision, when remaining unknowns can be recorded as assumptions, or when the user asks to stop. Do not force an endless interview. If critical evidence is unavailable, recommend a discovery step rather than false precision.
6. **Synthesize, do not execute.** Produce the brief and suggested next decisions. Do not automatically design, build, purchase, publish, or change systems unless separately requested.

Documents, analytics, research, or selected external sources may be used when provided or useful, but no source, tool, integration, or sibling skill is mandatory.

## Expected Output

During the interview, provide a short understanding update followed by two or three questions. At completion, produce:

- **Problem and audience**
- **Outcome and success measures**
- **In scope / out of scope**
- **Constraints, dependencies, and risk posture**
- **Evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions**
- **Candidate approach or next decision**, including validation needed

Keep uncertainty visible. A brief may be provisional when evidence is missing.

## Synthetic Example

User: "Plan a saved-search feature for analysts. We need a pilot this quarter, web only; notifications are out of scope."

Do not ask about timing, platform, or notifications again. Ask: (1) what repeated search task currently costs analysts the most time, (2) whether saved searches are personal or shared, and (3) what pilot behavior would count as success. If the user says personal-only, fewer repeated query setups, and a small internal cohort, reflect those constraints and ask only about material retention, permissions, or measurement gaps before drafting the brief.

## Boundaries

- Do not invent customer evidence, constraints, budgets, deadlines, or stakeholder agreement.
- Do not turn discovery into a coding pipeline or treat a requested feature as proof of a user problem.
- Do not require exhaustive answers; label assumptions and offer a reversible next step.
- Do not reveal private deliberation; provide concise questions, rationale where useful, and the resulting brief.
