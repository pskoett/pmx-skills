# Generic-Pattern Pass

After the quality pass, check whether generic model habits have displaced the user's observed voice. This is an editing pass, not an AI-authorship detector.

Look for:

- Empty introductory phrases and importance claims that postpone the point.
- Vague authorities such as "experts agree" without identifiable support.
- Dramatic reveals, rhetorical question stacks, or repeated "not X, but Y" contrasts that add no meaning.
- Synonym cycling that makes a single subject harder to follow.
- Repeated punch-line paragraphs, artificial symmetry, and slogan endings.
- Decorative headings, emphasis, emoji, or bullet lists unsupported by the format or profile.
- Generic courtesies or engagement prompts that conflict with the user's samples and purpose.

For each occurrence, ask whether it serves this piece and matches the observed frequency in comparable samples. Keep supported stylistic choices. Trim overuse instead of creating a universal blacklist; do not erase the user's distinctive voice just because a model can imitate it.

Keep deliberate quotations intact. Do not remove needed uncertainty or qualifications in the name of sounding human. Finish by comparing the draft with a relevant sample: does the rhythm match without copying its content or becoming a caricature?

When asked whether a text "sounds AI-generated", describe observable patterns, quote the lines, and suggest fixes. Explain that style alone cannot establish authorship; do not provide a fabricated probability or detector score. Rewrite only if requested.
